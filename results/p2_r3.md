## How the official code counts success (checked in the code)

It is cumulative. A pair counts as a success if it reached the goal at any MPC iteration so far.
- In `planning/mpc.py`, `self.is_success = self.is_success | successes`, and `action_len` records the step of the first success.
- `_apply_success_mask` zeroes the later actions of pairs that have already succeeded.
- `evaluator.eval_actions(actions, action_len)` judges each pair at its first-success step: `_get_traj_last(e_states, action_len * frameskip + 1)`.
- The final eval in `plan.py` (`perform_planning`, `final_eval/success_rate`) uses the same `action_len`.
- The loop stops only when all pairs have succeeded or `max_iter` is reached. plan_wall.yaml sets `max_iter: null`, which `mpc.py` turns into `np.inf`.
- In this run, the logged per-iteration success rate equals the cumulative count: 27/50 = 0.54, 44/50 = 0.88, 46/50 = 0.92, 46/50 = 0.92.

## What the paper states about the 0.96 (checked in arXiv 2411.04983v2)

- Table 1 (Sec. 4.3) and Appendix Table 8 report MPC on Wall as 0.96, over 50 sampled initial and goal states.
- Appendix A.5.1(f) says the loop repeats "until success is achieved or after a fixed number of iterations that we set as hyperparameter". No value is given.
- **Not stated:** the number of MPC iterations, the actions executed per iteration, the episode length or step limit, and any time limit for Wall.
- How success is counted (any step or the final step) is not stated either; the code above counts it cumulatively.

| Planner | Successes | Rate (%) | Wilson 95 % CI (%) | Paper | Stopped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MPC-CEM (plan_wall.yaml) | 46/50 | 92.0 | 81.2–96.8 | 0.96 (Table 1) | after 4 MPC iterations (our 300-min limit; ~75 min per iteration on a T4) |
| Plain CEM, open loop (conf/planner/cem.yaml) | 14/50 | 28.0 | 17.5–41.7 | 0.74 (Table 8) | after the 30 CEM optimisation steps (finished; 190.8 min) |

- Cumulative successes after MPC iterations 1–4: 27, 44, 46, 46 of 50. The paper's 0.96 lies inside our CI.
- The paper does not state its iteration limit (checked in `results/p2_r3.md`), so "after 4 iterations" is our stopping point, not the paper's.
- **Plain CEM: 14/50 (28%, CI 17.5–41.7); the paper reports 0.74, outside our CI, so this does not reproduce.** Run `vla-p2-r3cem` (commit `d158e21`, fixed settings: cem.yaml merged as the official sweep does; `results/p2_r3_cem.md`). The official `final_eval/success_rate` is 0.28.
- The official code also evaluates after every CEM step (`eval_every: 1`). The rate was 0.0, 0.02, 0.24, 0.48, 0.46, 0.44, 0.48, 0.52, 0.58, 0.54 over steps 1–10, identical to MPC-CEM's first iteration (same pairs, same optimisation). It peaked at 0.58 (step 9) and fell to 0.28 by step 30: more optimisation steps lowered the real success rate. We report the final step, per the protocol, and do not pick a step by its result.
